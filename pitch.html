Slide 1 — Problem

Airfield lights are still replaced reactively. We need to score degradation before failure.

ADB Safegate's Axon EQ fixtures stream telemetry into CORTEX, but maintenance still happens after a light fails — at airport scale that means safety risk, runway disruption, and wasted spend.

Safety risk

A failed runway or taxiway light is an immediate operational hazard.

Unplanned downtime

Emergency dispatches and runway closures are far more expensive than scheduled maintenance.

Wasted spend

Replacing healthy fixtures too early is as costly as replacing failed ones too late.

The data catch

The thing we want to measure — LED degradation — is not directly observable. No photometric output, no hours-run counter, no labeled failures, no Arrhenius-grade junction temperature.

Our mission

Build a per-fixture degradation indicator from existing telemetry, defended by LED and driver physics rather than labels.

Slide 2 — Our algorithm

Four physics-grounded features, ranked within circuit, combined into a score.

F1 — Boost level

Trimmed mean of boostVoltage. Sustained V_f elevation under the CCR constant-current assumption.

F2a — Envelope magnitude

Median of non-zero (V_max − V_min) per window. Driver ripple amplitude during active instability.

F3 — Level drift

Theil-Sen slope of per-batch boost level. V_f drift rate, controlling for commissioning offset.

F4 — Envelope drift

Theil-Sen slope of per-batch envelope. Capacitor degradation rate — leading indicator.

Within-circuit normalization (mandatory — Cohen's d = 1.01) Symmetric LED ⊕ driver sub-scores Confidence from window count & stale fraction

CCR support voltage

$$V_{\text{boost}} \approx \sum V_f + V_{\text{driver losses}}$$

If loop current is held ~constant, a higher required forward-voltage support shows up as elevated boost voltage.

Driver ripple / envelope

$$\Delta V_{\text{ripple}} \approx \frac{I_{\text{load}}}{C \cdot f_{sw}} + I_{\text{ripple}} \cdot \text{ESR}$$

Capacitance loss or ESR growth widens the boost-voltage envelope — the driver-side leg.

Slide 3 — Scope

What the indicator says, and what it deliberately does not claim.

✅ It can say

  • Relative degradation ranking per fixture, within circuit.
  • Coherent co-occurrence of boost level + envelope widening + drift on top-decile fixtures.
  • Driver-side early warning (F2a, F4) for capacitor wear before LED V_f drift dominates.
  • Confidence flags where window count is low or stale fraction is high.
  • Suppression of weak-evidence / RF-coupled fixtures in a negative-control lane.

❌ It cannot say

  • Remaining useful life in hours — no runtime counters.
  • Lumen / photometric loss — optical output is not measured.
  • Absolute failure dates — no labeled failures to calibrate against.
  • Arrhenius-calibrated aging — LED junction temperature is sentinel-flat (led01/02T = 25 °C, heater = −55 °C).
  • Cross-circuit comparison in raw units (Cohen's d = 1.01 offset).

Slide 4 — From defensible to production-ready

Six telemetry streams close the gap between physics-grounded prototype and predictive maintenance.

1. inputCurrent

Verifies the CCR constant-current assumption that the entire boost-voltage leg rests on.

2. LED junction temperature

Real T_j (not the 25 °C sentinel) unlocks the Arrhenius acceleration model below.

3. Photometric output

Connects electrical stress to actual lumen loss — the quantity ADB Safegate ultimately cares about.

4. Runtime / hours-run counter

Separates calendar age from usage age; lets the score normalize against duty cycle.

5. Failure & maintenance labels

Calibrate the symmetric LED ⊕ driver weighting (currently an explicit assumption) and validate ranking.

6. RF health metrics

Build a true negative-control lane instead of the current synthetic one based on stale activity.

Arrhenius — the model we cannot run yet

$$k_{\text{degradation}} = A \cdot e^{-\frac{E_a}{k T_j}}$$

Correct physics for thermal acceleration, but available data has no real junction temperature, so we do not claim Arrhenius-calibrated degradation today.

With these streams, the indicator moves from ranked triage list to calibrated remaining-useful-life estimate.
