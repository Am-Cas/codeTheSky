Case Study 2 – LED Degradation

Reactive replacement → Physics‑grounded preventive maintenance

Current reactive maintenance causes safety risk, unplanned downtime, and unnecessary cost.

Data reality

What we have

  • Electrical telemetry: boostVoltage, stale, lastSeen, limited env (humidity, pressure)
  • No photometric output, no runtime counters, no failure labels
  • Many signals are flat or sentinel‑filled (LED temps, heaters, magnetic)

What we lack

  • Input current (CCR assumption)
  • LED junction temperature
  • Maintenance / failure history
  • Optical performance data

CCR support voltage

$$V_{\text{boost}} \approx \sum V_f + V_{\text{driver losses}}$$

If loop current is held ~constant, a higher required forward-voltage support shows up as elevated boost voltage.

Driver ripple / envelope

$$\Delta V_{\text{ripple}} \approx \frac{I_{\text{load}}}{C \cdot f_{sw}} + I_{\text{ripple}} \cdot \text{ESR}$$

Capacitance loss or ESR growth widens the boost-voltage envelope — the driver-side leg.

Physics‑grounded indicator

How our algorithm works

  • LED side: boost voltage level (F1) & drift (F3) capture increased forward‑voltage as LEDs age.
  • Driver side: non‑zero envelope magnitude (F2a) & drift (F4) capture capacitor wear and ripple widening.
  • Score per circuit: rank features, combine LED and driver sub‑scores symmetrically, add confidence from window count & stale fraction.

What it tells us

  • Relative degradation ranking per fixture.
  • Confidence flags where data is sparse or stale.

What it cannot tell

  • Exact remaining useful life (hours) – no runtime counters.
  • Optical output loss – no photometric data.
  • Absolute failure dates – no labeled failures.

LED side

As LEDs age, defect accumulation and series resistance can increase required forward-voltage support. We capture this with boost-voltage level and level drift.

Driver side

Power-chain stress and capacitor wear can widen or worsen boost-voltage envelope behavior. We capture this with non-zero envelope magnitude and envelope drift.

Scoring

F1: boost level F2a: non-zero envelope F3: level drift F4: envelope drift

CCR support voltage

$$V_{\text{boost}} \approx \sum V_f + V_{\text{driver losses}}$$

If current is held approximately constant, a higher required LED forward-voltage support appears as elevated boost voltage.

LED diode relationship

$$I = I_s \left( e^{\frac{q V_f}{n k T_j}} - 1 \right)$$

With current constrained, changes in junction condition, defect density, and series resistance can shift the required forward voltage.

Driver ripple / envelope

$$\Delta V_{\text{boost}} = V_{\text{max}} - V_{\text{min}}$$
$$\Delta V_{\text{ripple}} \approx \frac{I_{\text{load}}}{C \cdot f_{sw}} + I_{\text{ripple}} \cdot ESR$$

Capacitance loss or ESR growth can widen the boost-voltage envelope, which motivates the driver-side score.

Thermal aging model, not used for scoring

$$k_{\text{degradation}} = A \cdot e^{-\frac{E_a}{k T_j}}$$

This is the correct physics for temperature acceleration, but the available data lacks real LED junction temperature, so we do not claim Arrhenius-calibrated degradation.

Slide 3 — Scope

What the indicator says, and what it deliberately does not claim.

✅ It can say

  • Relative degradation ranking per fixture, within circuit.
  • Coherent co-occurrence of boost level + envelope widening + drift on top-decile fixtures.
  • Driver-side early warning (F2a, F4) for capacitor wear before LED V_f drift dominates.
  • Confidence flags where window count is low or stale fraction is high.
  • Suppression of weak-evidence / RF-coupled fixtures in a negative-control lane.

❌ It cannot say

  • Remaining useful life in hours — no runtime counters.
  • Lumen / photometric loss — optical output is not measured.
  • Absolute failure dates — no labeled failures to calibrate against.
  • Arrhenius-calibrated aging — LED junction temperature is sentinel-flat (led01/02T = 25 °C, heater = −55 °C).
  • Cross-circuit comparison in raw units (Cohen's d = 1.01 offset).

Honest gap analysis

What we still need for production

  • inputCurrent – verify constant‑current assumption.
  • LED junction temperature – enable Arrhenius‑based aging model.
  • Photometric output – link electrical stress to lumen loss.
  • Runtime counters – separate calendar time from usage.
  • Failure / maintenance labels – calibrate and validate scores.
  • RF health metrics – build true negative‑control lane.

With these streams the indicator moves from defensible prototype to production‑ready predictive maintenance.

Value now

A ranked list for maintenance review, with confidence labels and suppression for weak evidence.

Drill in

Defend
